Project 7: Student Habits vs. Academic Performance

CS110 · Spring 2026 · Yawen Lei

Overview

This project uses Python and pandas to analyze the relationship between student lifestyle habits and academic performance. The dataset contains information on 1,000 students across 16 variables including study hours, sleep, social media usage, diet quality, exercise frequency, and exam scores.

What It Does

  • Loads a real dataset from Kaggle using kagglehub
  • Explores the dataset structure: column types, missing values, basic statistics
  • Filters and compares student groups based on study habits
  • Uses vectorized pandas operations to calculate counts, percentages, and group averages
  • Draws conclusions about what habits correlate with higher exam scores

Key Concepts

  • pandas DataFrames: Loading, inspecting, and slicing tabular data with .head(), column selection, and boolean filtering
  • Vectorization: Applying operations to entire columns at once rather than looping row by row — for example, (df['study_hours_per_day'] > 6).sum()
  • Boolean indexing: Filtering rows that meet a condition and computing statistics on those subsets
  • Group comparison: Splitting students into groups and comparing their average exam scores to find meaningful patterns

Key Findings

Question Result
Students studying more than 6 hours/day 40 students (4.4%)
Students studying less than 2 hours/day 13.5%
Average exam score (study > 5 hrs/day) 91.1
Average exam score (study ≤ 5 hrs/day) 65.7

Students who studied more than 5 hours per day scored on average 25 points higher than those who studied less — a substantial gap that suggests study time is one of the strongest predictors of exam performance in this dataset.

Code Highlight

# Compare average exam scores between high and low study-hour groups
more_than_5 = df[df['study_hours_per_day'] > 5]['exam_score'].mean()
less_than_5  = df[df['study_hours_per_day'] <= 5]['exam_score'].mean()

print("Average exam score (study > 5 hrs):", more_than_5)   # 91.1
print("Average exam score (study ≤ 5 hrs):", less_than_5)   # 65.7

What I Learned

I was surprised by how much you can find in a dataset with just a few pandas operations. Vectorization makes filtering and aggregating data fast and readable, with no loops needed. Splitting students into two groups and comparing their averages was a simple approach, but it surfaced a pretty clear pattern between study time and exam performance.
