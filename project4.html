Project 4: NLP Text Analysis

CS110 · Spring 2026 · Yawen Lei

Overview

This project uses Python and spaCy to analyze word frequency patterns in Jane Austen's Pride and Prejudice, fetched from Project Gutenberg. The goal was to compare a basic word count approach against a linguistically cleaned version to see which reveals more meaningful information about the text.

What It Does

  • Fetches the full text of Pride and Prejudice from an online source and caches it locally
  • Counts raw word frequency using a basic tokenization approach
  • Processes the same text with spaCy: removes stop words and punctuation, applies lemmatization
  • Compares the top 15 words from each approach side by side

Key Concepts

  • NLP (Natural Language Processing): Using spaCy to linguistically process text rather than treating it as raw strings
  • Lemmatization: Reducing words to their base form so "said," "saying," and "says" all count as "say"
  • Stop word removal: Filtering out common filler words like "the," "and," and "of" that appear frequently but carry little meaning
  • Word frequency analysis: Counting how often each meaningful word appears to surface themes and characters

Results

The difference between the two approaches was striking. The basic count surfaced only grammatical filler — "the," "to," "of," "and" — at the top. After cleaning with spaCy, the top 15 words became almost entirely character names and meaningful verbs:

What I Learned

Raw word counts are misleading — they are dominated by grammatical structure rather than content. Once stop words are removed and words are lemmatized, the results immediately tell a story: the top words are character names, and the most common verbs ("say," "know," "think") reflect how dialogue-heavy and psychologically focused Austen's writing is. NLP tools like spaCy make it possible to surface that layer of meaning quickly and systematically.
